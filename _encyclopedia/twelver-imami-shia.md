### Definition:

The Twelver Imami Shia are a sect of Muslims who claimed that Ali was more deserving of succeeding to the Caliphate than the two Shaykhs (Abu Bakr and Umar) and Uthman, may Allah be pleased with all of them. They were called "Imamiyyah" because they made the Imamate the central issue occupying their thought, and "Twelver" (Ithna 'Ashariyyah) because they believed in twelve Imams, the last of whom entered the cellar in Samarra, according to their claim. Furthermore, they stand as the opposing faction to Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jama'ah in their distinct thought and opinions, working to spread their doctrine across the Islamic world.

### Foundation and Prominent Figures:

* **The Twelve Imams** whom the Imamiyyah take as their leaders follow this lineage:
* **Ali ibn Abi Talib**, whom they title *Al-Murtada* (The Pleased/Chosen One) — the fourth of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, son-in-law of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. He died a violent death when the Kharijite Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam assassinated him in the Mosque of Kufa on the 17th of Ramadan in the year 40 AH.
* **Al-Hasan ibn Ali**, whom they title *Al-Mujtaba* (3–50 AH).
* **Al-Husayn ibn Ali**, whom they title *Al-Shahid* (The Martyr) (4–61 AH).
* **Ali Zayn al-Abidin ibn al-Husayn** (38–95 AH), whom they title *Al-Sajjad* (The Prostrater).
* **Muhammad al-Baqir ibn Ali Zayn al-Abidin** (57–114 AH), whom they title *Al-Baqir* (The Splitter/Revealer of Knowledge).
* **Ja'far al-Sadiq ibn Muhammad al-Baqir** (83–148 AH), whom they title *Al-Sadiq* (The Truthful).
* **Musa al-Kazim ibn Ja'far al-Sadiq** (128–183 AH), whom they title *Al-Kazim* (The Restrainer of Anger).
* **Ali al-Rida ibn Musa al-Kazim** (148–203 AH), whom they title *Al-Rida* (The Pleased/Satisfied).
* **Muhammad al-Jawad ibn Ali al-Rida** (195–220 AH), whom they title *Al-Taqi* (The Pious/God-fearing).
* **Ali al-Hadi ibn Muhammad al-Jawad** (212–254 AH), whom they title *Al-Naqi* (The Pure).
* **Al-Hasan al-Askari ibn Ali Abd al-Hadi** (232–260 AH), whom they title *Al-Zaki* (The Pure/Virtuous).
* **Muhammad al-Mahdi ibn al-Hasan al-Askari** (256 AH – ...), whom they title *Al-Hujjah Al-Qa'im Al-Muntazar* (The Proof, The Risen, The Expected).

They claim that the twelfth Imam entered a cellar in his father’s house in *Surra Man Ra'a* (Samarra) and never returned. They disagreed on his age at the time of his disappearance; some say four years old, while others say eight. However, most researchers conclude that he never existed at all and that he is a fabrication of the Shia (*), referring to him as *Al-Ma'dum* (The Non-Existent) or *Al-Mawhum* (The Imagined).

* **Historical figures** among them include:
* **Abd Allah ibn Saba'**: A Jewish man from Yemen who feigned Islam and imported concepts from Jewish thought into Shiism—such as the doctrine of *Al-Raj'ah* (Return), eternal life, ownership of the earth, possessing supernatural abilities beyond all creation, possessing hidden knowledge, and attributing *Al-Bada'* (divine alternation/change of will) and forgetfulness to Allah Almighty (Exalted is He far above what they claim). In his Judaism, he used to say that Joshua the son of Nun was the legatee of Moses, peace be upon him; thus, in Islam, he claimed that Ali was the legatee of Muhammad, peace be upon him. He traveled from Medina to Egypt, Kufa, Fustat, and Basra, and said to Ali: "You are You!" (meaning you are God), which prompted Ali to order his execution. However, Abd Allah ibn Abbas advised him not to do so, so Ali exiled him to Al-Mada'in.
* **Mansur Ahmad ibn Abi Talib al-Tabarsi** (died 588 AH), author of the book *Al-Ihtijaj*, printed in Iran in 1302 AH.
* **Al-Kulayni**, author of the book *Al-Kafi*, printed in Iran in 1278 AH. Among them, it holds the same status as *Sahih al-Bukhari* does for Ahl al-Sunnah (*), and they claim it contains 16,199 narrations.
* **Hajj Mirza Husain ibn Muhammad Taqi al-Nuri al-Tabarsi** (died 1320 AH), buried in the Al-Murtadawi Shrine in Najaf. He is the author of *Fasl Al-Kitab fi Ithbat Tahrif Kitab Rabb Al-Arbab*, in which he claims that the Quran was subjected to addition and omission. Examples of this include their claim that Surah Al-Inshirah is missing the phrase: *"And we made Ali your son-in-law"*—God forbid that such a claim be true. This book was printed in Iran in 1289 AH.
* **Ayatollah Al-Mamqani**, author of *Tanqih Al-Maqal fi Ahwal Al-Rijal*, whom they regard as the master of critic and authenticator (*Jarh wa Ta'dil*). In it, he refers to Abu Bakr and Umar by the titles *Al-Jibt* and *Al-Taghut* (see Vol. 1, p. 207, printed 1352 at the Murtadawi Press in Najaf).
* **Abu Ja'far al-Tusi**, author of *Tahdhib Al-Ahkam*; **Muhammad ibn Murtada, known as Mullah Muhsin al-Kashi**, author of *Al-Wafi*; **Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Hurr al-Amili**, author of *Wasa'il Al-Shia ila Ahadith Al-Shari'ah*; **Muhammad Baqir ibn al-Shaykh Muhammad Taqi, known as Al-Majlisi**, author of *Bihar Al-Anwar fi Ahadith al-Nabi wa al-A'immah al-Athar*; **Fath Allah al-Kashani**, author of *Manhaj Al-Sadiqin*; and **Ibn Abi al-Hadid**, author of *Sharh Nahj Al-Balagha*.
* **Ayatollah Khomeini**: One of the contemporary Shia (*) figures. He led a Shia revolution in Iran that assumed state power and authored books such as *Kashf Al-Asrar* and *Islamic Government*. He formulated the concept of *Wilayat al-Faqih* (Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist). Although he raised general Islamic slogans at the beginning of the revolution (*), he soon revealed a narrow, fanatical Shia tendency and a desire to export his revolution to the rest of the Islamic world, taking measures that—along with other factors—led to an eight-year war with Iraq.

### Thoughts and Beliefs:

* **The Imamate**: It is established by divine designation (*nas*). The preceding Imam must explicitly designate the succeeding Imam by specific identity, not merely by general description. They hold that the Imamate is too vital a matter for the Prophet, peace be upon him, to have departed and left the community unguided, letting every individual follow their own opinion. Instead, he had to appoint a specific person to be the ultimate reference and pillar.
* They argue that the Prophet peace be upon him explicitly designated Ali as Imam after him in a manifest declaration on the Day of Ghadir Khumm—an event that neither the narrators nor the historians of Ahl al-Sunnah (Sunnis) authenticate.
* They claim that Ali explicitly designated his two sons, Hasan and Husayn, and so on—every Imam appointing his successor by a final testament, whom they call the *Wasi* (Legatees).

* **Infallibility**: All Imams are infallible, protected from error, forgetfulness, and the commission of both major and minor sins.
* **Ladunni Knowledge (Divine/Illicit Knowledge)**: Every Imam has been endowed with knowledge directly from the Messenger (*) peace be upon him to complete the Shariah. He possesses *Ladunni* knowledge, and there is no difference between him and the Prophet except that revelation is not sent down to him. The Messenger of Allah entrusted them with the secrets of the Shariah so they could explain to the people what their respective times required.
* **Miraculous Feats**: It is permissible for extraordinary feats to occur at the hands of an Imam, which they call a miracle (*). If there is no explicit designation from the preceding Imam, proof of the Imamate in such cases must be established through these extraordinary feats.
* **Occultation (*Ghaybah*)**: They believe that time is never devoid of a proof from Allah, both logically and legally. Consequently, they claim the twelfth Imam went into hiding inside his cellar, experiencing a Lesser Occultation and a Greater Occultation—all of which belong to their myths.
* The Return (*Raj'ah*) (*) : They believe that Al-Hasan al-Askari will return at the end of time when Allah permits him to emerge. Some of them used to stand after sunset at the door of the cellar leading a saddled mount, chanting his name and calling him to emerge until the stars intertwined, after which they would disperse and postpone the matter to the following night. They claim that upon his return, he will fill the earth with justice just as it was filled with oppression and tyranny, and he will exact retribution from the adversaries of the Shia (*) throughout history. The Imamiyyah unanimously affirm *Raj'ah*, while some of their other sub-sects affirm the return of certain other deceased individuals as well.
* Dissimulation (*Taqiyyah*) (*) : They regard it as a pillar of religion; whoever abandons it is like someone who abandons prayer. It is obligatory and cannot be lifted until the arrival of *Al-Qa'im*. Whoever discards it before his emergence has exited the religion of Almighty Allah and the religion of the Imamiyyah. They cite as proof the verse: *"Except that you prudently guard yourselves against them"* (Quran 3:28). They attribute to the fifth Imam, Abu Ja'far, the saying: *"Taqiyyah is my religion and the religion of my ancestors, and there is no faith for one who has no Taqiyyah."* They expand the concept of Taqiyyah to an extreme degree.
* **Mut'ah (Temporary Marriage)**: They view temporary marriage as the best of customs and most virtuous acts of devotion, citing the verse: *"So whatever you enjoy [of marriage] from them, give them their compensation as an obligation"* (Quran 4:24). Islam prohibited this form of marriage which stipulates a fixed time limit, whereas most Sunni scholars require the explicit intention of permanence. Temporary marriage carries numerous negative societal consequences that justify its prohibition.
* **Belief in the "Mushaf of Fatima" (1)**: Al-Kulayni narrates in his book *Al-Kafi* (p. 57, 1278 AH edition) on the authority of Abu Basir (i.e., Ja'far al-Sadiq): *"And with us is the Mushaf of Fatima, peace be upon him."* He said: *"I asked, 'What is the Mushaf of Fatima?' He said, 'A Mushaf containing three times the volume of this Quran of yours; by Allah, not a single letter of your Quran is in it.'"*
* **Disavowal (*Bara'ah*)**: They disavow the first three Caliphs—Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman—and describe them with the ugliest epithets because, as they claim, they usurped the Caliphate from Ali, who was more deserving of it. They initiate matters by cursing Abu Bakr and Umar instead of saying the *Basmala* in every important affair. They likewise attack many of the Companions with curses and do not hesitate to slander the Mother of the Believers, Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her.
* **Extremism (*Ghuluw*)**: Some of them exaggerated the status of Ali, may Allah be pleased with him. The extremist faction among the Shia (*), such as the Saba'iyyah (*), elevated him to the rank of divinity. Others claimed that the Angel Gabriel made a mistake in delivering the message and descended to Muhammad, peace be upon him, instead of Ali, because Ali resembled the Prophet just as a crow resembles a crow; hence they were called the *Ghurbiyyah*.
* **The Festival of Ghadir Khumm**: An annual festival of theirs coinciding with the 18th of Dhu al-Hijjah. They prefer it over Eid al-Adha and Eid al-Fitr, calling it the "Greater Festival." Fasting on this day is a confirmed Sunnah among them, marking the day they claim the Prophet appointed Ali to the Caliphate (*) after him.
* **Veneration of Nowruz**: They venerate Nowruz, which is a Persian festival, and some state that ritual bathing on Nowruz is a Sunnah.
* **The Festival of 9th of Rabi' al-Awwal**: They hold a festival on the 9th of Rabi' al-Awwal, known as the festival of *Baba Shuja' al-Din*—a title they gave to *Abu Lu'lu'a al-Majusi*, the assassin of Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him.
* **Mourning Rituals**: They conduct ceremonies of grief, wailing, chest-beating, carrying imagery, and numerous forbidden acts during the first ten days of Muharram, believing these actions draw them closer to Allah and expiate their sins and misdeeds. Anyone who visits their sacred shrines in Karbala, Najaf, Qom, and elsewhere will witness astonishing sights of this.

### Intellectual and Doctrinal Roots:

* **Persian Influence**: Shiism absorbed the beliefs of the Persians, who historically revered divine monarchy and hereditary succession. The Persians contributed to it in order to exact revenge against Islam—which had shattered their empire—using the framework of Islam itself.
* **Syncretism**: Shia thought intermingled with imported doctrines from Asian philosophies such as Buddhism, Manichaeism, and Brahmanism, leading to beliefs in metempsychosis (reincarnation) and divine incarnation (*hulul*).
* **Jewish Heritage**: Shiism derived concepts from Judaism, which itself carried Assyrian and Babylonian pagan footprints.
* **Christological Parallels**: Their statements concerning Ali ibn Abi Talib and the Imams of the Prophet's household parallel Christian views regarding Jesus, peace be upon him. They also mirror Christians in their abundance of festivals, extensive use of imagery, fabrication of extraordinary feats, and attributing them to their Imams.

### Spread and Spheres of Influence:

Twelver Imami Shiism is currently widespread in Iran, where it is concentrated. A large population resides in Iraq, extending into Pakistan, alongside a community in Lebanon. In Syria, there is a small community closely tied to the Nusayris (Alawites), who belong to the extremist Shia sects.

### Summary:

Shiism began as a political party advocating the preferential right of Ali ibn Abi Talib to the Caliphate. It subsequently evolved into a doctrinal and political sect that sheltered anyone seeking to undermine Islam and the Muslim state. A student of Islamic history will find that nearly every revolt, secession from the central state, or doctrinal crisis had the various Shia factions behind it or involved in it. Consequently, Islamic history became stained with numerous civil conflicts and fragmentation. Due to infiltrated elements within the Muslim ranks whose interests lay in perpetuating this division, the crisis never ended. The rift persisted to the point where Shiism nearly became a religion entirely distinct from Islam. Western circles and Orientalists capitalized on this division to portray Muslims as warring sects and factions, drawing comparisons to Christianity and its hundreds of denominations.
