### Definition:

The Mu'tazilah are an Islamic sect that emerged in the late Umayyad period and flourished during the Abbasid era. They relied on pure, abstract reason to understand Islamic doctrine, having been influenced by imported philosophies, which led them to deviate from the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jama'ah. They were known by various names, including *Al-Mu'tazilah*, *Al-Qadariyyah*, *Al-Adliyyah*, *Ahl al-Adl wa al-Tawhid*, *Al-Muqtasidah*, and *Al-Wa'idiyyah*.

### Foundation and Prominent Figures:

* **Origins of Mu'tazilism:** Scholars hold two main views regarding the emergence of the Mu'tazila:
1. **The First View:** The movement arose from debates on theological issues, such as the status of the major sinner and the question of divine decree (*Qadar*)—specifically, whether human beings have absolute agency over their actions. Proponents differ on how they earned the name "Mu'tazilah":
* They "withdrew" from the mainstream Muslim community by introducing the doctrine of the "intermediate position" (*al-manzila bayn al-manzilatayn*).
* They became known as such after Wasil ibn Ata withdrew from the study circle of Al-Hasan al-Basri and formed his own circle after asserting the intermediate position, prompting Al-Hasan to remark, "Wasil has withdrawn from us."
* They asserted the obligation to boycott and dissociate from the major sinner.


2. **The Second View:** The emergence had political roots. The Mu'tazilah—originally partisans (shia) of Ali—withdrew their support from Al-Hasan when he abdicated in favor of Mu'awiya, or they adopted a neutral stance between the camps of Ali and Mu'awiya, thereby withdrawing from both.


* **Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar al-Hamadhani** (the historian of the Mu'tazilah) claimed that Mu'tazilism was neither a new sect nor an invented faction, but rather a continuation of the tradition of the Prophet and his Companions. He argued that the name stemmed from their withdrawal from evil, citing the Quranic verse: *"And I will withdraw from you and what you invoke besides Allah"* (Quran 19:48), and the prophetic tradition: *"Whoever withdraws from evil falls into good."*
* **Historical Reality:** The rise of Mu'tazilism was the product of a historical evolution of intellectual and theological principles born from abstract rational speculation applied to religious texts, heavily influenced by Greek and Indian philosophies, as well as Jewish and Christian doctrines.
* **Early Polemical Foundations:** Before emerging as a distinct intellectual sect under Wasil ibn Ata, early polemical arguments laid the groundwork for Mu'tazili thought:
* **Absolute Human Free Will:** The doctrine that humans possess absolute free will and create their own actions was articulated by Ma'bad al-Juhani (who revolted alongside Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Ash'ath and was executed by Al-Hajjaj in 80 AH) and later by Ghaylan al-Dimashqi during the reign of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz (executed by Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik).
* **The Created Quran and Denial of Attributes:** Advanced by Jahm ibn Safwan (executed in Merv, 128 AH) and Al-Ja'd ibn Dirham (executed by Khalid ibn Abd al-Qasri in Kufa).


* **Emergence Under Wasil ibn Ata:** The Mu'tazilah crystallized as an intellectual sect under Wasil ibn Ata al-Ghazal (80–131 AH), a student of Al-Hasan al-Basri who broke away after declaring that the major sinner occupies an intermediate position (*al-manzila bayn al-manzilatayn*—neither a believer nor a disbeliever) and is condemned to eternal Hellfire if unrepentant before death. The faction originating from him is called the *Wasiliyyah*.
* **Sectarian Fragmentation:** Relying heavily on reason in theology, the Mu'tazilah fractured into various sub-sects—while agreeing on five core principles—with each faction introducing new innovations and adopting the name of its founder.
* **Under the Abbasids:** Mu'tazilism gained state sponsorship under Caliph Al-Ma'mun, influenced by Bishr al-Marisi, Thumama ibn Ashras, and Ahmad ibn Abi Du'ad (chief architect of the trial regarding the created Quran and Chief Judge under Al-Mu'tasim).
* During the **Inquisition (*Mihnatu Khalq al-Quran*)**, Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal refused to submit to the Caliph's decree affirming the created Quran. He was imprisoned, tortured, and flogged under Al-Mu'tasim, remaining incarcerated for two and a half years before being confined to his home through the reigns of Al-Mu'tasim and Al-Wathiq.
* When **Caliph Al-Mutawakkil** assumed power in 232 AH, he championed Ahl al-Sunnah (*), honored Imam Ahmad, and ended fourteen years of Mu'tazili state dominance and ideological coercion.


* **Under the Buyid Dynasty:** In 334 AH, the Shiite Buyid dynasty in Persia fostered close ties with the Mu'tazilah, elevating their status. Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar was appointed Chief Judge of Rayy in 360 AH by Al-Sahib ibn Abbad, a pro-Shia Mu'tazili minister. Al-Dhahabi described him as an innovator with Shiite and Mu'tazili leanings. Al-Maqrizi noted that Mu'tazilism spread widely under Buyid rule in Iraq, Khorasan, and Transoxiana. Prominent figures like Al-Sharif al-Murtada also emerged during this era as staunch Twelver Imami scholars versed in speculative theology and Mu'tazilism.
* **Prominent Mu'tazili Thinkers:**
* **Abu al-Hadhail Hamdan al-Allaf (135–226 AH):** Head of the Basran Mu'tazila, influenced by Greek philosophers like Aristotle and Empedocles, asserting that "Allah knows by a knowledge which is His very essence, and is powerful by a power which is His very essence..." (His followers: *Hudhailiyyah*).
* **Ibrahim ibn Yasar al-Nazzam (died 231 AH):** Influenced by Brahmanism and Greek philosophy, known for his theory of *tafrah* (leap) and denying the indivisible particle (His followers: *Nazzamiyyah*).
* **Bishr ibn al-Mu'tamir (died 226 AH):** Introduced extreme views on generation (*tawakkuld*), claiming humans generate colors, tastes, and smells (His followers: *Bishriyyah*).
* **Mu'ammar ibn Abbad al-Sulami (died 220 AH):** Noted for extreme views in denying divine attributes and pre-destination (*) (His followers: *Ma'amariyyah*).
* **Isa ibn Sabih al-Murdar (died 226 AH):** Dubbed the "Monk of the Mu'tazilah," known for sweeping excommunication (*takfir*), declaring the entire community—including other Mu'tazilites—as disbelievers (His followers: *Murdariyyah*).
* **Thumama ibn Ashras (died 213 AH):** A hedonistic court figure who allegedly incited Al-Ma'mun toward Mu'tazilism (His followers: *Thumamiyyah*).
* **Amr ibn Bahr al-Jahiz (died 256 AH):** A master prose writer who subtly embedded Mu'tazili doctrine in literary works like *Al-Bayan wa al-Tabyin* (His followers: *Jahiziyyah*).
* **Abu al-Husayn al-Khayyat (died 300 AH):** Claimed that non-existent entities are physical bodies, implying the eternity of the universe (His followers: *Khayyatiyyah*).
* **Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar (died 414 AH):** Chief Judge of Rayy, master systematizer who codified Mu'tazili doctrine.

### Principles and Doctrines:

The Mu'tazilah systematized their theology around **Five Principles (*Al-Usul al-Khamsa*)**:

1. **Tawhid (Divine Unity):** Allah is entirely transcendent, lacking any peer or likeness (*"There is nothing like unto Him"*). However, they built false corollaries upon this—such as declaring the beatific vision impossible, denying divine attributes as distinct from the essence (fearing multiplicity of the eternal), and claiming the Quran is created due to rejecting Allah's attribute of speech.
2. **Al-'Adl (Divine Justice):** They asserted that Allah does not create human actions, desires no evil, and only commands what He wills and prohibits what He detests. They claimed He never burdens souls beyond their capacity, confusing His universal will (*kawniyyah*) with His legislative will (*shar'iyyah*).
3. **Al-Wa'd wa al-Wa'id (The Promise and the Threat):** Allah must reward the righteous and punish the wicked, never forgiving a major sinner without repentance.
4. **Al-Manzila bayn al-Manzilatayn (The Intermediate Position):** A major sinner is neither a believer nor a disbeliever, but an unrepentant transgressor destined for eternal Hellfire (while remaining culturally termed a "Muslim" outwardly).
5. **Al-Amr bi al-Ma'ruf wa al-Nahy 'an al-Munkar (Enjoining Good and Forbidding Evil):** Obligatory on believers by tongue, hand, or sword—which justified armed rebellion against rulers deemed unjust or deviant.

#### Methodological Deviations:

* **Absolute Rationalism:** Treating reason as the sole ultimate arbiter of good and evil prior to divine revelation (*husn wa qubh ‘aqliyyah*).
* **Metaphorical Distortion (*Ta'wil*):** Reinterpreting scriptural texts concerning divine attributes (istiwa, hand, eye, divine pleasure and anger) to fit rationalist premises.
* **Slandering the Companions:** Questioning the integrity and historical verdicts of senior Companions (e.g., claiming one of the two warring factions at the Battle of the Camel consisted of transgressors whose testimony was inadmissible).

### Intellectual Roots and Foreign Influences:

* **Jewish & Indian Roots:** Tracing the denial of attributes back through Jahm ibn Safwan and Al-Ja'd ibn Dirham, influenced by Jewish polemicists and Indian religious groups (such as the *Samaniyyah* transmigrationists).
* **Christian Polemics:** Influence from John of Damascus regarding libertarian free will and denial of eternal attributes.
* **Greek Philosophy:** Heavy borrowing from Empedocles and Aristotle regarding divine essence and attributes, atomism, and spatial leaps (*tafrah*).
* **Subterranean Sectarian Milieu:** The pluralistic cultural backdrop of early Iraq (mixing Chaldean, Persian, Christian, Jewish, and Zoroastrian heritages), where converts integrated old metaphysical frameworks into Islamic theology.

### Modern Revive-ist / "Neo-Mu'tazili" Thought:

Contemporary writers and intellectuals attempt to revive Mu'tazili thought under banners like *rationalism*, *enlightenment*, *renewal*, or *Islamic Leftism*. Influenced by materialist Western philosophy, they subject scriptural texts to subjective rational filtering, reject physical miracles (e.g., Muhammad Abduh’s rationalizing of the Ababil birds in Surah Al-Fil as smallpox-carrying carriers), challenge established legal texts (such as apostasy laws, jihad, inheritance, polygyny, and corporal punishments), and prioritize modern Western socio-legal norms over divine legislation.
