### Definition:

The Ibadis (Al-Ibadiyyah) are one of the sects of the Kharijites (Khawarij), tracing their name to their founder, Abd Allah ibn Ibad al-Tamimi. While adherents claim they are not Kharijites and reject this label, the reality is that although they are not among the extremist Kharijites (such as the Azariqa), they agree with the Kharijites on numerous matters. Among these is that Abd Allah ibn Ibad considered himself an extension of the original *Muhakkima* of the Kharijites. They also align with the Kharijites in the denial of divine attributes (*ta'til*), the belief in the creation of the Quran, and the legitimization of rebelling against unjust imams.

### Foundation and Prominent Figures:

* **Founder:** Its primary founder, Abd Allah ibn Ibad, belonged to the Banu Murra ibn Ubayd ibn Tamim, tracing his lineage back to Ibad, a village in Al-Arid, Al-Yamama. Abd Allah lived during the era of Mu'awiya and passed away in the late period of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan.
* **Jabir ibn Zayd** (22–93 AH): Ibadis cite him as one of their most prominent figures and among the earliest engaged in compiling Hadith, having studied under Abd al-Nahhas (Abd Allah ibn Abbas), Aisha, Anas ibn Malik, Abd Allah ibn Umar, and other senior Companions—noting that Jabir ultimately dissociated himself from them (see *Tahdhib al-Tahdhib*, 2/38).
* **Abu Ubayda Muslim ibn Abi Karima:** One of Jabir ibn Zayd's most famous students who became the leading authority of the Ibadis after him, widely known by the nickname *Al-Qafaf*. He died during the reign of Abu Ja'far al-Mansur in 158 AH.
* **Al-Rabi' ibn Habib al-Farahidi:** Lived in the mid-second century AH and is attributed a specific compilation of hadith known as *Musnad al-Rabi' ibn Habib*, which is printed and widely circulated.
* **Imams in North Africa during the Abbasid era:** Imam Al-Harith ibn Talid, followed by Abu al-Khattab Abd al-A'la ibn al-Samh al-Ma'afiri, then Abu Hatim Ya'qub ibn Habib, and Hatim al-Malzuzi.
* **Imams of the Rustamid Dynasty in Tahert, Maghreb:** Abd al-Rahman, Abd al-Wahhab, Aflah, Abu Bakr, Abu al-Yaqzan, and Abu Hatim.
* **Prominent Scholars:**
* **Salama ibn Sa'd:** Spread their doctrine in Africa in the early second century.
* **Ibn Maqtir al-Janauni:** Studied in Basra and returned to his homeland in Jabal Nafusa, Libya, to contribute to spreading the Ibadi doctrine (*).
* **Abd al-Jabar ibn Qais al-Muradi:** Served as a judge during the time of Imam Al-Harith ibn Talid.
* **Al-Samh Abu Talib:** A scholar of the latter half of the second century AH; served as vizier to Imam Abd al-Wahhab ibn Rustam, then as his governor over Jabal Nafusa and its surroundings in Libya.
* **Abu Dhar Aban ibn Wasim:** A scholar of the first half of the third century AH; served as governor for Imam Aflah ibn Abd al-Wahhab over the region of Tripoli.

### Thoughts and Beliefs:

* **Denial of Divine Attributes (*Ta'til*):** Their texts reveal a denial of the divine attributes (*), aligning closely with the Mu'tazila in metaphorical interpretation (*ta'wil*) of these attributes. They claim to approach this from a theological standpoint, interpreting attributes metaphorically to preserve meaning without leading to anthropomorphism (*tashbih*). However, the correct stance remains that of Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jama'ah (*), who follow the textual evidence by affirming Allah's supreme names and attributes exactly as He affirmed them for Himself—without *ta'til* (denial), *taklif/takyif* (describing how), *tahrif* (distortion), or *tamthil* (resemblance).
* **Denial of the Beatific Vision:** They deny that believers will see Allah in the Hereafter, despite explicit Quranic texts to the contrary: *"Faces that Day will be radiant, looking toward their Lord"* (Quran 75:22–23).
* **Metaphorical Interpretation of the Hereafter:** They interpret certain eschatological matters metaphorically, such as the Scale (*Mizan*) and the Bridge (*Sirat*).
* **Human Actions:** They hold that human actions are created by Allah and acquired by the individual, placing them in a middle ground between the Qadariyya (*) and the Jabriyya (*).
* **Divine Attributes:** They hold that Allah's attributes are not additional entities over and above His Essence, but rather identical to His Essence.
* **The Created Quran:** They maintain that the Quran is created, agreeing with the Kharijites (*). Al-Ash'ari stated: *"All the Kharijites say the Quran is created"* (*Maqalat al-Islamiyyin*, 1/203).
* **Status of the Major Sinner (*Murtakib al-Kabira*):** A major sinner is considered an ingrate in terms of blessings (*kufr ni'ma*) or a hypocrite in terms of hypocrisy (*kufr nifaq*), rather than disbelieving in the core creed (*kufr milla*).
* **Classification of People:** They divide people into three categories:
1. Believers faithful to their faith.
2. Explicit polytheists in their polytheism.
3. People who declared the word of Tawhid and professed Islam but failed to adhere to it in conduct and worship. They are not polytheists because they confess Tawhid, nor are they believers because they fail to fulfill the requirements of faith. Thus, in worldly rulings, they are treated alongside Muslims due to their profession of Tawhid; but in the rulings of the Hereafter, they are aligned with the polytheists for failing to fulfill their faith and violating what Tawhid demands in action or omission.

* **The Abode (*Dar*):** While contemporary Ibadi hadith scholars hold various views regarding "the Abode," they generally agree with early authorities that the domain of their dissenting Muslim peers is considered an *Abode of Tawhid* (Dar Tawhid), with the exception of a sultan's military camp, which they deem an *Abode of Tyranny* (Dar Baghhy).
* **Status of Dissenting Muslims:** They view non-Ibadi Muslims of the *Qiblah* as disbelievers (though not polytheists). Intermarriage and inheritance with them are permissible, and seizing their wealth—specifically weapons, horses, and military equipment—is considered lawful, while anything else is forbidden.
* **Eternal Punishment for Major Sinners:** Anyone who commits a major sin and persists in it without repentance cannot enter Paradise. Allah does not forgive major sins unless repented before death. While Ahl al-Sunnah view such a person as a disobedient sinner subject to Allah's will (who may forgive him out of grace or punish him justly before eventually admitting him to Paradise), the Ibadis assert that such a sinner is eternally condemned to Hell, aligning with the Kharijites and Mu'tazila.
* **Denial of Intercession:** They deny intercession for sinful monotheists because, in their view, sinners are eternally in Hell and thus ineligible for any exit.
* **Conditions of the Imamate:** They do not mandate that the Imam must be from the Quraysh; any qualified righteous Muslim is eligible. An imam who deviates must be deposed and replaced.
* **Selection of the Imam:** The Imamate cannot be transmitted by will or lineage; selection occurs exclusively through *Bay'ah* (pledge of allegiance). Multiple imams can exist simultaneously in different regions.
* **Rebellion Against Unjust Rulers:** They neither mandate nor prohibit rebelling against an unjust ruler; rather, they permit it. If circumstances are favorable and potential harms are minimal, this permissibility leans toward obligation. If conditions are unfavorable and risks are high, it leans toward prohibition. Nevertheless, rebellion is never completely barred under any condition, and *shira'* (concealment/clandestine opposition) is preferred whenever the ruler is unjust.
* **Restrictions on Supplication:** It is impermissible for someone to pray for another regarding the blessings of Paradise unless that person is a Muslim faithful to their religion and deserving of loyalty through obedience. However, praying for worldly well-being or transition from worldly to heavenly status is permissible for any Muslim, whether righteous or sinful.
* **Halaqat al-Azzaba:** A restricted administrative body consisting of the most knowledgeable, righteous, and pious individuals in a town. It exercises full supervision over the religious, educational, social, and political affairs of the Ibadi community, serving as a consultative council during times of openness and defense, and acting on behalf of the Imam during periods of concealment (*shira'*).
* **Iruwan:** An auxiliary consultative organization assisting the *Azzaba*, functioning as the second highest authority in the town.
* **Zakat Committees:** They form committees dedicated to collecting Zakat and distributing it to the poor, strictly forbidding begging or soliciting aid.
* **Splinter Groups:** Several sub-sects broke away from the Ibadis and eventually vanished, including:
* **Hafsiyya:** Followers of Hafs ibn Abi al-Miqdam.
* **Harithiyya:** Followers of al-Harith al-Ibadi.
* **Yazidiyya:** Followers of Yazid ibn Anisa, who claimed Allah would send a prophet from the non-Arabs with a new heavenly book, thereby abandoning the Shariah of Muhammad, peace be upon him. The mainstream Ibadis dissociated themselves from these splinter groups, declaring them disbelievers due to their extreme deviations.

### Intellectual and Doctrinal Roots:

* **Hadith Reliance:** They rely heavily in matters of Sunnah upon what they call *Musnad al-Rabi' ibn Habib*—a compilation that verified scholars have proven to be unauthentic.
* **Zahiri Influence:** They are influenced by the Zahiri (literalist) school, often taking a rigid, literalist approach to religious texts.
* **Mu'tazili Influence:** They absorbed the Mu'tazili doctrine regarding the creation of the Quran.
* **Key Reference:** *Al-Nil wa Shifa' al-Alil*, annotated by Shaykh Muhammad ibn Yusuf Itfayyish (died 1332 AH), stands as one of their most prominent references, compiling Ibadi jurisprudence and theology.

### Spread and Spheres of Influence:

* Historically active across southern Arabia, reaching as far as Makkah and Madinah.
* In North Africa, their doctrine spread among the Berbers, establishing the Rustamid State centered in Tahert. They ruled independently for about 130 years until overthrown by the Fatimids (Ubaydids).
* An independent Ibadi state arose in Oman, where Ibadi imams maintained governance down to modern times.
* Historical centers include Jabal Nafusa in Libya, which served as their stronghold and administrative hub.
* Present-day existence persists notably in Oman (with a high percentage of the population), Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Saharan oases, and Zanzibar (which was integrated into Tanganyika under the name Tanzania).

### Summary:

The Ibadis are an offshoot of the Kharijites, named after their founder Abd Allah ibn Ibad al-Tamimi. While adherents reject the "Kharijite" label, and while they avoided the extreme violence of groups like the Azariqa, they share core premises with the Kharijites—such as tracing their origins to the early *Muhakkima*, denying divine attributes (*ta'til*), asserting the creation of the Quran, and validating rebellion against unjust rulers.
