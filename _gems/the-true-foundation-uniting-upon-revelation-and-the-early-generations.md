King Salman bin Abdulaziz—may Allah protect him—said:

It is appropriate to clarify to the reader that some writings confuse the description of the da'wah as 'Wahhabism' with other terms, believing that the matter is merely about terminology. In reality, the da'wah of Sheikh Muhammad ibn Abdulwahhab—may Allah have mercy on him—which was supported and championed by the founding Imam, Muhammad bin Saud—may Allah have mercy on him—and continued by his sons and descendants to this day, is nothing other than a call to return to the principles of the Islamic religion as set forth in the Quran and the noble Prophetic Sunnah.

Any fair-minded person can examine the letters and writings of Sheikh Muhammad ibn Abdulwahhab to realize that there is nothing new in that da'wah that contradicts the Quran, the Sunnah, or the methodology of the Salaf. It is simply a call to return to the correct foundations of pure Islamic creed, which serve as its basis and starting point.

As for what writer Khalil Al-Khalil mentioned—that the term 'Wahhabism' is analogous to what is attributed to the four schools of jurisprudence—this in my view is incorrect, because saying this is equivalent to saying that what the Sheikh brought is a new school of thought, whereas the Sheikh relied in his fatwas and opinions upon the four madhhab (schools of thought). What the writer mentioned, that 'Wahhabism' has become another face of Salafism, is also incorrect, because true Salafism is what "the righteous predecessors" (al-Salaf al-Salih) were upon in terms of a methodology committed to the Quran and the Sunnah, and the da'wah of Sheikh Muhammad ibn Abdulwahhab is a return to that methodology and did not use Salafism for partisan or nominal purposes as has become widespread in our modern era, unfortunately.
