### Question: 

The brother A. M. M. from Kuwait says in his question: "One of the scholars mentioned that Imam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) viewed the celebration of the Prophet's Birthday (Mawlid) as something commendable. Is this true, O Your Eminence the Sheikh?"

### Answer: 

Celebrating the Prophet's Birthday (Mawlid)—upon its owner be the best of prayers and purest of salutations—is an innovation (bid'ah) that is not permissible according to the most correct opinion of the scholars. **This is because the Prophet ﷺ did not do it, nor did his Rightly-Guided Caliphs, nor all of his Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), nor the scholars and rulers during the three preferred centuries.** Rather, it occurred after that time because of the Shiites and those who imitated them, so it is permissible neither to practice it nor to imitate those who do.

And Sheikh Taqi al-Din Ahmad ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) is among those who reject this and view it as bid'ah (an innovation). However, in his book "Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim Mukhalafat Ashhab al-Jahim," he mentioned regarding those who do it out of ignorance. 

It is not fitting for anyone to be deceived by those people who practice it, endorse it, or call to it—such as Muhammad Alawi al-Maliki and others—because the proof does not lie in the statements of men; rather, the proof lies in what Allah the Glorified said, what His Messenger ﷺ said, or what the predecessors of the Ummah agreed upon. 

This is based on the saying of Allah the Exalted: **“O you who believe! Obey Allâh and obey the Messenger (Muhammad ﷺ), and those of you (Muslims) who are in authority. (And) if you differ in anything amongst yourselves, refer it to Allâh and His Messenger (ﷺ), if you believe in Allâh and in the Last Day. That is better and more suitable for final determination.”** [An-Nisa': 59]; 

His saying the Glorified: **"“And in whatsoever you differ, the decision thereof is with Allâh (He is the ruling Judge).”** [Ash-Shura: 10]; 

And His saying the Glorified: **“Indeed in the Messenger of Allâh (Muhammad ﷺ) you have a good example to follow for him who hopes for (the Meeting with) Allâh and the Last Day, and remembers Allâh much.”** [Al-Ahzab: 21].

And he (upon him be peace and blessings) did not do that, and he conveyed the clear message through his words and actions ﷺ, and his Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) did not do it either. Had it been a good thing, they would have preceded us to it.

The Prophet ﷺ said: **"Whoever introduces into this matter of ours (Islam) something that is not part of it will have it rejected"** [Agreed upon by Bukhari and Muslim] [^1].

He (upon him be peace and blessings) also said: **"Whoever performs an action that is not in accordance with our matter [Islam], it will be rejected"** (Reported by Muslim in his Sahih) [^2].

He ﷺ used to say in his sermons: **"To proceed: The best speech is the Book of Allah, the best guidance is the guidance of Muhammad ﷺ, the worst of matters are those newly invented, and every bid'ah (innovation) is a misguidance"** (Reported by Muslim in his Sahih) [^3].

And the Hadiths bearing this meaning are numerous. I have written at some length regarding this matter, as well as concerning other innovations—such as the celebrations of the Night of Al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj (Night Journey and Ascension) and the middle of Sha'ban.

All of these have been published in a booklet entitled Al-Tahdhir min al-Bida' (Warning Against Innovations), which is distributed by the General Presidency of Scholarly Research and Ifta and the Ministry of Islamic Affairs. It is also available in my book entitled Majmoo' Fatawa wa Maqalat (Collection of Fatwas and Articles), volume 1, page 227. Whoever wishes to review it may do so.

We ask Allah to grant success to us and to all Muslims in knowing the truth and following it, and to protect us all from innovations and evil deeds, both apparent and hidden. Indeed, He is the Guardian of that and is capable of it. May Allah send peace and blessings upon our Prophet Muhammad, his family, and his Companions.

====

Sheikh Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymeen (may Allah the Most High have mercy on him) said: 

As for someone who is ignorant [of the ruling], he does not incur sin, because none of the acts of disobedience incur sin except when accompanied by knowledge. He may even be rewarded for his good intention, as Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah pointed out in his book Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim. Thus, he is rewarded for his intention, not for his action. His action is neither righteous, nor accepted by Allah, nor pleasing to Him; however, due to his good intention coupled with ignorance, he receives a reward. This is why the Prophet ﷺ said to the man who prayed and then repeated his ablution after finding water and prayed a second time: **'You will have a reward twice'** [^4] —due to his good intention, and because the action itself is fundamentally a righteous deed. However, if someone were to intentionally perform an action a second time while knowing it is unlegislated, he would not receive a reward, because his action is unlegislated since it goes against the Sunnah. Indeed, the Prophet ﷺ said to the one who did not repeat it: **'You have hit upon the Sunnah.'** End quote. [^5]

Sheikh Rabi' (may Allah the Most High have mercy on him) said: 

As for the statement of Ibn Taymiyyah in Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim [^6]: "What some people introduce—either emulating the Christians in the birth of Jesus (peace be upon him), or out of love for the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) and veneration. And Allah may reward them for this love and effort, not for the innovations of taking the birthday of the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) as a festival"—this means the reward is for the love; He rewards them for their love for the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him), not for the action. This mitigates the issue, because those [others] who quote Ibn Taymiyyah believe that Allah rewards them for this action. Why? Because it stems from love for the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him). Here, Shaykh al-Islam explicitly states that Allah does not accept this innovator's action, but rather He may reward them for the love. However, even regarding this love that motivated them to disobey the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him), they are not rewarded for it [in that context]. Their love for the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) in general, outside of this occasion, will benefit them, in sha' Allah. But this unlegislated love that drove them to practice these innovations—they are not rewarded for it, nor is any honor granted for it. Indeed, Shaykh al-Islam mentioned in another place that the utmost that can be said is that the ignorant person among them is excused, yet he loses this action and it is not accepted from him. Everyone's statement is to be accepted or rejected. Meaning, even if it is Shaykh al-Islam, evidence must be brought for his statement. Everyone's statements are examined through evidence, except for the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him). Shaykh al-Islam himself says that men are not to be taken as the standard of proof; rather, proof is brought for them [^7]. So if a man comes to you with a statement, say to him: "Where is your evidence? Bring your proof?" If he has no proof, his statement is not accepted—may Allah forgive him, pardon him, and we pray for him. But by Allah, it is not permissible; it is forbidden to follow him in error. End quote. [^8]

Sheikh Salih Al Shaykh (may Allah the Most High preserve him) said in his audio lecture Al-Sunnah wal-Bid'ah: 

They also said: "Shaykh al-Islam—meaning Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him)—says in a statement of his in Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim, while discussing the revival or celebration of the night of the Prophet's birth (upon him be peace and blessings): After clarifying that it is an innovation (bid'ah), he said: 'And among the people are those who do that and are rewarded for what has settled in their hearts of love for the Prophet (upon him be peace and blessings).'" They argue: "This indicates that a person is rewarded for those celebrations." The response to this is: Shaykh al-Islam himself is the one who ruled that this act is an innovation. His statement that there are those who do that and are rewarded does not mean they are rewarded unconditionally; rather, they are rewarded for what has established itself in their hearts of love. Allah the Majestic and Exalted establishes the just balance, and the angels record everything. Thus, the meaning of Shaykh al-Islam's statement is that whoever performs that act is rewarded from one perspective—which is the perspective of love—because Allah the Majestic and Exalted does not wrong anyone in the least. However, he is burdened with sin from the perspective of the action itself, for his action is an innovation, and he is blamed for it because he introduced an innovation. As for the origin of love, this is not something he performed by way of innovation; rather, what occurred by way of innovation are the celebrations themselves. Therefore, Shaykh al-Islam was precise in his statement: the evaluation of deeds before Allah the Majestic and Exalted entails that you receive credit for what you do of righteous deeds, and you are held accountable for what you commit of sins. Whoever has goodness established in his heart is rewarded for it, but he incurs sin for the action that he innovated. An innovation—as is well-known in the statements of the scholars—is worse than ordinary acts of disobedience, meaning major sins driven by desires. Why? Because major sins and sins of desire are committed by a person while he knows he is disobedient. However, the practitioner of innovation continues to perform [his act] thinking he is obeying Allah the Majestic and Exalted and His Messenger (upon him be peace and blessings), thus having introduced a matter into the religion, whereas the Prophet (upon him be peace and blessings) said: **"Every newly invented matter is an innovation, every innovation is a misguidance, and every misguidance is in the Fire."**. Consequently, there is a division between the action itself and what settles in the heart. What settles in the heart regarding the origin of love—this has the ruling of its general category of deeds regarding the reward for it, according to the statement of Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah. As for what settles in the heart of perceiving innovations as good, believing that what contradicts the Sunnah is correct, and the external performance of celebrations and the like—this is a misguiding innovation because it is newly invented in the religion and because the definition of an innovation fully applies to it. End quote.

#### A Useful Point:

The prominent scholar Salih al-Fawzan (may Allah preserve him) said: 
***"If it is necessary to quote the statements of the scholars, one must quote them fully from beginning to end, gather the scholar's statements on the issue from his various books so that his intent becomes clear, refer some of his statements back to others, and not suffice with quoting just one part while leaving out the other. For this causes misunderstanding and attributes to the scholar what he never intended."*** End quote [^9].
